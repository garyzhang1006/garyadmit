# Product

## Register

product

## Users

High-school seniors applying to selective US colleges, usually alone at a laptop in the evening during application season, pasting a personal statement or supplement they have already rewritten many times. Parents and counselors use it with them. A review takes one to three minutes; then they spend thirty to sixty minutes reading the feedback, editing, and coming back to earlier reviews to compare versions.

The job: find out honestly how good the essay is and what to change first, then change it without losing their own voice.

## Product Purpose

GaryAdmit gives a score out of 100 that can be checked: two independent readers, seven category scores, line edits, and blind comparisons against real published essays. It then offers a checked rewrite and a chat for targeted changes, each verified by a blind judge before it is called better. One button also makes the line edits at once, writes any detail an edit asks for, and says which edits it could not make. The editor is asked to mark each one as made up, and a check looks for any it missed, so the student replaces them with the truth.

Success is a student who trusts the score, knows the three things to fix first, and leaves with a stronger draft that still sounds like them.

## Brand Personality

Candid, exact, kind. The tagline is "No glaze." Feedback is direct and specific, never cruel and never hyped. The interface should feel like a trusted reader's desk late at night: calm under pressure, serious about evidence, and respectful of a teenager's writing.

## Anti-references

- The AI editorial template: cream paper, Fraunces or Newsreader headlines, uppercase mono microlabels, a rotated rubber stamp, hard offset shadows. This was the first version of this UI and the reason for the redesign.
- SaaS dashboards: hero metric cards, gradient accents, identical card grids.
- Grammarly-style clutter: every line underlined, red everywhere, a sidebar of nagging cards.
- Gamified college-prep apps: confetti, badges, streaks, cheerleading copy.
- Chatbot skins: purple gradients and glowing avatars.

## Design Principles

1. The essay is the page. Interface chrome recedes, and the student's words get the best typography on screen.
2. Evidence over adjectives. Every claim carries its quote, its judge, or its comparison, one click away and never in the way.
3. Calm honesty. Bad news is stated plainly, and color marks meaning rather than mood.
4. One job per view. Reading feedback, fixing lines, rewriting, and checking the evidence are separate places, each short enough to finish.
5. Long waits are accounted for. Every multi-minute job says what it is doing and lets the student keep working elsewhere.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.2 AA in both light and dark themes: 4.5:1 contrast for body text, visible focus on every control, full keyboard use (tabs, disclosures, chat, history drawer), no meaning carried by color alone (results and severities are also written out), `prefers-reduced-motion` honored everywhere, and text that zooms to 200% without horizontal scrolling. Touch targets are at least 44px on small screens.
